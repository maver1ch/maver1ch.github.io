## Problem

Building small AI automations was not the hard part. Getting the first client was.

- **No proof** — a new profile with zero jobs and zero reviews.
- **Crowded** — every job gets a wave of proposals within minutes.
- **Silence** — my first 10 proposals were never opened.

A client scanning that list decides in seconds. So my proposal had to win the click in **15 words**: the client's problem in their own words, and that I was **ready to start now**. Once they opened it, a **demo** of most of the job showed I could already do it.
</ArchitectureSection>

<PhaseMarker label="Phase 2 — Win: proof before the contract" highlight />

<DecisionBlock
  decision="Spend the first 15 words on the client's problem."
  why="That is all a client reads before choosing whom to open. My proposals got short: their problem in their words, then a clear 'I can start now'."
  tradeoff="My background waits until the client opens the proposal."
/>

<DecisionBlock
  decision="Build a demo of about 70% of the job before being hired."
  why="A working demo answers the client's real question, can this person do it, faster than any profile. It shows I am ready and that I care about their problem."
  tradeoff="Unpaid hours on jobs I might not win."
/>

<PhaseMarker label="Phase 3 — Keep: deliver like they are my only client" />

- **Replies in minutes** — online in the client's hours, so no question waited.
- **Daily updates** — a short note each day on what was done and what came next.
- **Advice past the brief** — when I saw a risk or a better way outside the job post, I said so.
- **Clean handover** — code with notes, so their team could run it without me.

## Outcome

- **First client in May 2026**, after 10 proposals that were never opened.
- **3 jobs in 13 days** for the same client, **3 reviews**, **$400+** earned.
- **Customer obsession won it, not technical depth** — the client's problem first, proof before the contract, care after it.
